# <TICKET> PO baseline brief (shared by every agent in this review)

<TICKET_SUMMARY>

All paths are relative to `<REPO>/.ai/<TICKET>/` unless absolute.

## The two steps
1. **PO baseline.** <PO> (product owner, PO) wrote or steered `<PO_DOC>` and the Jira description, bringing their own knowledge of the client to both. Those two texts are a stronger signal than the call transcript. Infer what the PO understood and decided, and keep it apart from what was merely said on the call and from what Claude drafted. Output: `review/po-baseline.md`.
2. **Deviation check** (only once `prd.md` has a draft). Compare the PRD against the baseline. Output: `review/deviations.md`.

## Sources of truth for "what the PO decided", highest first
1. **<PO>'s own explicit answers:**
   - doc comment threads they wrote (replies marked "via mcp" were generated by Claude under their account, so they are weaker);
   - their Jira comments (`jira/comment_*.md`);
   - answer lines they typed under questions in the description. Check authorship and timing against the changelog in `jira/inventory.md`.
2. **The doc body** `<PO_DOC>`, written by the PO with Claude.
3. **The Jira description's notes and summaries** (`jira/description.md`).
4. **The call transcript** `<TRANSCRIPT>`: what was actually said.
5. **Prior client context:** <PRIOR_CONTEXT>.

**Not baseline:** the developer's interview decisions in `prd.md`. Where they differ from the baseline they are deliberate developer choices that need PO confirmation, not errors.

**Feasibility (code truth):** <FEASIBILITY_SOURCES> and the repo `<REPO>` (its `CLAUDE.md` holds the connector decisions). Code wins over docs.

## Attribution classes (step 1)
| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **T** | Said on the call, carried over faithfully |
| **P** | PO direction: added, changed, narrowed, resolved or dropped relative to the call, with no sign it's mere drafting |
| **C** | Likely Claude drafting: structure, wording, guessed columns, inference with no PO signal |
| **X** | The sources contradict each other (doc vs description vs call vs comments) |
| **—** | Silence: no source decides it. Whatever the PRD picks here is the developer's choice |

Every item carries a confidence (H/M/L) and short evidence: a quote of at most 20 words plus its location.

**Citation shorthand:** `call m:ss` (transcript timestamp) · `doc:N` (line in `<PO_DOC>`) · `desc:N` (line in `jira/description.md`) · `JC:N` (the PO's Jira comment) · `Fr` (the PO's doc-thread answers) · `Desc-Q` (the PO's answer lines in the description).

## Finding format (step 2)
`ID · location (R-id / section) · class · as written · baseline says (evidence) · why it matters (scope, estimate, client expectation) · suggested resolution`. End with a **Confirmed OK** list showing what you checked.

## Hard rules
- **Read-only** on all sources, `prd.md` and every repo. Write only your own output file(s) under `review/`.
- Never write to Jira, Confluence, the client's systems or any live system. Data probes go through `<SANDBOX_CALL>` read-only: counts and value shapes, never customer PII.
- Don't edit people profiles; report observations in your return instead.
- Never Read PNG/JPEG files (they can hold customer data, and an image in your context is re-sent on every later call).
- A silence stays a silence: don't fill gaps with best practice.

## Output: `review/po-baseline.md` (the synthesizer writes it)
1. **How to read this:** the shorthand table; the authority order with the real timestamps of each PO text; the **sequencing finding** (which text is newest, and whether newest really is the latest decision); **inputs that left no record** (meetings mentioned but never transcribed).
2. **Baseline table** `#1…#n`, grouped by topic: `# · Topic · Baseline (what the PO expects) · Class · Conf · Evidence`.
3. **PO decisions** `PO-1…PO-n` (class P, confidence H/M), quoted **in the PO's words**.
4. **Agreed on the call and carried over** (T).
5. **Not baseline:** Claude drafting with no PO endorsement (C).
6. **Open or contradictory** `X-1…X-n`, each phrased as a question for the PO with options and the way the evidence leans; then the list of silences.
7. **Facts the PO may not have had:** data and code facts that bear on the PO's decisions (e.g. (FFD-14): 551 core-charge products in the client's CSV, an existing Miva field that made a requested new tag unnecessary).

## Output: `review/deviations.md` (the deviation checker writes it)
**Classes**
| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ALIGNED | The PRD matches the baseline |
| CONTRADICTS-PO | The PRD contradicts a PO-n decision |
| FACT-RISK | A "fact the PO may not have had" undermines a requirement |
| NARROWING | The PRD does less than the PO expects |
| MISSING | A baseline item the PRD lacks |
| EXPANSION (S/M/L) | The PRD goes beyond the PO and the call; S/M/L is the cost weight |
| RESOLVES-X | The PRD settles an X-n |

**Reading rule:** a deviation is a confirm-or-change item for <PO>, not a defect. Silence items are developer choices by definition; list them only when they add cost or touch a client expectation.

**Sections:** 1 Headline (a count table by class; how many R-ids are aligned, aligned with one part flagged, or developer choices; how many PO-n are reflected; how many X-n are settled; the 5 most consequential deviations) · 2 Findings by class, `DV-1…DV-n` in the finding format · 3 **Questions for <PO>**, deduplicated and phrased for a PO (no class names or code terms), plus the read-only developer checks to run before asking · 4 Confirmed OK (ALIGNED) · 5 Classification ledger (every R, non-goal and baseline row → class).

## Per-agent prompts (orchestrator)
Spawn B0, B1, the synthesizer and the deviation checker as `sn-writer` agents, and B2 as `sn-scout`.

B0, B1 and B2 run in parallel. No transcript (`<TRANSCRIPT>` is "none"): skip B0; B1 compares the description, the PO doc and the comments, and the synthesizer reads B1 and B2. The synthesizer starts after every completion notice. The deviation checker runs after the synthesis, and only once a PRD draft exists.

**B0, control (transcript only).** "Read the shared brief first: `review/baseline-BRIEF.md`. You are B0, the control. Read **only** `<TRANSCRIPT>`: no Jira, no `prd.md`, no research, no code. Be literal: if the call didn't settle something, say so. Don't fill gaps with best practice. Write `review/baseline/B0-transcript-only.md` with: 1 Agreed (each with a timestamp and a quote of at most 20 words) · 2 Proposed but not agreed · 3 Explicitly rejected or out of scope · 4 Ambiguities and contradictions within the call · 5 What the call never covered that a feature of this shape needs · 6 Topics that look like separate work. Start with the transcript caveats you resolved: mis-heard names (e.g. (FFD): 'Neva/Meva' = Miva, 'course/four' = core) and speaker labels that look wrong. Return ≤15 lines." *Why a control:* whatever B0 can't derive from the call but the PO's doc contains is either PO direction or Claude drafting.

**B1, delta.** "Read the shared brief first. You are B1. Don't read `prd.md` or `research/`. Topic by topic, compare the call vs the doc vs the description vs the PO's comments, and tag each delta T/P/C/X, or 'silence' for something dropped. Topics: <TOPIC_LIST>.
- Start with a **sequencing table**: every PO-authored text with its timestamp from `jira/inventory.md` and the doc's revision history. Newest is not automatically the latest decision: a later summary of the call can restate as open what the PO had already decided.
- Read **silence as signal**: what the PO dropped from the call matters.
- Use **authorship tells**: the PO's own typing habits (e.g. (FFD-14): recurring misspellings that Claude-drafted text never has) mark the PO's text; replies marked 'via mcp' were written by Claude under the PO's account.
Write `review/baseline/B1-delta.md`: Sequencing · Topic table (`# · topic · call · doc · description · comments · class · conf · evidence`) · <PO>'s direction in their own words · Contradictions they left unresolved · Tally by class. Return ≤15 lines."

**B2, prior context and PO lens.** "Read the shared brief first. You are B2. Don't read `prd.md`. Establish what <PO> knew before the call. Read <PRIOR_CONTEXT> and their profile `~/.claude/people/<PO_SLUG>.md` (Review lens, Positions). Data counts come from the client files or `<SANDBOX_CALL>` (counts and shapes only). Write `review/baseline/B2-prior-context.md`: 1 What the client said before the call, per source · 2 Prior decisions and facts that bear on <TICKET> · 3 The PO's lens as documented (cite the profile) · 4 Implications for reading the PO's doc: what may be carried-over knowledge rather than the call · 5 Facts the PO may not have had. Return ≤15 lines."

**Synthesizer.** "Read the shared brief first. Read B0, B1 and B2 **and** the primary sources they cite (transcript, doc, description, comments, `jira/inventory.md`). Don't read `prd.md`: the baseline must stay independent of the draft. Settle disagreements between B0–B2 **with evidence, not by vote**: re-read the primary source for every conflict. Quote PO decisions verbatim and never paraphrase a decision-shaping phrase (FFD-14: 'please review X' became 'build on X' in a paraphrase, which changed the scope). Write `review/po-baseline.md` per the brief. Return ≤20 lines: counts per class, PO-n and X-n counts, the top 5 facts the PO may not have had."

**Deviation checker.** "Read the shared brief first, then `review/po-baseline.md` and `prd.md` (Draft). Re-read the primary sources wherever a finding turns on them. Classify every R, every non-goal and every baseline row. Remember the reading rule: a deviation is a confirm-or-change item for <PO>, not a defect. Write `review/deviations.md` per the brief. End with the deduplicated questions for <PO>, phrased for a PO, and the read-only developer checks to run before asking. Return ≤20 lines: counts by class and the top 5."

## Orchestrator checks after the synthesis
- **Verbatim quotes:** grep the exact text of the top PO-n quotes in the sources (FFD-14: 6 of 6 held).
- **Paraphrase drift:** re-read the PO's words for every decision-shaping claim; fix a drifted paraphrase with an exact replace.
- **Data claims:** recount them against the data (CSV, `<SANDBOX_CALL>`) before they reach the PO.
- **Feed the interview:** tag each option "PO decided X" / "silence" / "expansion, cost S/M/L", and put the ticket- or PO-literal option first. On FFD-14, untagged options led to 15 expansions nobody chose consciously.
